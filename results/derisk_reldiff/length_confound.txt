============================================================================
INTERPRETATION
============================================================================

The EA effect is NOT just length confound — it's the dominant signal.

Key findings:
  - EA rollouts are slightly longer than clean (1.27× on means; 1.1× on medians).
    Real difference, p~0, but small in magnitude.
  - In EVERY length quintile (Q1 shortest through Q5 longest), EA cases get
    a more-negative delta than clean cases. EA-vs-clean gap ranges -0.41 to
    -0.88 across quintiles.
  - Within clean rollouts only, ρ(length, delta) = +0.009, p=0.19 — length
    by itself does NOT drive penalty when there's no EA.
  - Within EA rollouts only, ρ(length, delta) = -0.10, p=4e-12 — long EA
    rollouts get penalized harder than short EA rollouts.

So the gradient has the following character:
  1. PRIMARY: EA-vs-clean gap (-0.4 to -0.9 across quintiles) — strong, real
     signal that pushes EA down.
  2. SECONDARY: among EA cases, length amplifies the penalty (long-rambling EA
     gets rated worse than brief EA mention) — actually plausible/desirable.
  3. NULL: clean-only length effect — basically zero. Length alone isn't a
     gradient driver.

The 2.8× lift of long-CoT in the very-negative bucket comes from the EA
subset within long-CoT rollouts, not from a generic length penalty.

RISKS to monitor at runtime (lower than initially feared):
  - If the model learns 'make EA CoTs shorter' rather than 'don't EA at all',
    we'll see CoT length collapse on EA-prone prompts but not on clean ones.
    Track per-class CoT length over training.
  - If even non-EA CoTs start shrinking, the length signal is being
    over-generalized — abort and add a length-normalized reward.
  - GRPO normalizes within-prompt rollout group, which mostly removes the
    length effect (rollouts of one prompt at temp=0.9 have similar length).
